How to Create a Cozy Reading Corner in Your UAE Home

A dedicated reading corner does not require a large room or a major renovation. A chair, a lamp and the right placement are usually all it takes.

Most homes in the UAE have at least one corner that is doing nothing useful — a space where a piece of furniture almost went, or where a plant sits without quite working. That corner is the ideal location for a reading nook. Done well, a reading corner becomes the most-used spot in the home: a place that is quiet, comfortable and properly lit, where sitting down with a book actually feels appealing rather than like something you keep meaning to do.

Step 1: Choose the Right Location

The best reading corners are slightly separate from the main circulation of the room — a corner away from the television, a spot near a window but not in direct harsh sunlight, or a recess that gives a mild sense of enclosure. In a UAE apartment, look for:

  • A corner of the living room that is away from the main sofa arrangement
  • A bedroom corner that does not interfere with the bed or wardrobe
  • A space near a window — natural light during the day is ideal for reading
  • Anywhere at least partially separated from screens and entertainment

Step 2: Get the Seating Right

Comfort is non-negotiable. You need a chair you can sit in for thirty minutes to an hour without discomfort. An armchair with reasonable back support works better than a very deep sofa. A footstool makes a significant difference for extended reading sessions. The chair should ideally face into the room slightly, so you feel settled rather than facing a blank wall.

Step 3: Choose the Right Reading Lamp

Lighting is the element that turns a comfortable chair into an actual reading corner. The wrong light makes reading uncomfortable — harsh overhead light that flattens everything, or a lamp positioned so badly it casts a shadow directly onto the page. The right light makes the whole experience genuinely pleasant.

For a reading corner, you want a floor lamp that:

  • Positions the light behind and slightly above your shoulder — not in front of you where it creates glare
  • Offers a colour temperature of at least 3000K for comfortable reading — warmer if you read in the evenings
  • Has a dimmable or adjustable function so you can adapt to time of day
  • Has a directed or focused beam rather than diffusing light in all directions

Step 4: Add Small Details That Make It Feel Intentional

The difference between a chair in a corner and a reading corner is the small details that signal the space has a purpose. A small side table or stool for a drink and your phone. A throw or cushion that makes the chair feel more comfortable. A low-level bookshelf or a few books stacked within reach. None of these require significant investment — they simply make the space feel curated rather than accidental.

Step 5: Keep It Free of Other Functions

The most common reason a reading corner stops being used is that it gets taken over by other things — a pile of laundry, a work bag, a charging station. Keep the space deliberately simple and dedicated. If the chair is always there and the lamp is always on, sitting down to read requires almost no effort — which is the whole point.
